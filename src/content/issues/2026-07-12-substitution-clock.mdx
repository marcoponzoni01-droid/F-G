An export control is usually reported as a denial. It is better understood as a
tax on time.

Almost nothing on a control list is physically unobtainable. It is obtainable
more slowly, from fewer suppliers, at worse yields, and at a price that includes
the cost of proving provenance. The controlled party does not stop; it
substitutes. So the only question that has ever mattered is how long substitution
takes — and that is the one number nobody puts in the announcement.

## What moved

Equities repriced on the announcement as though the denial were permanent.
Suppliers of the controlled input lost a quarter of their market capitalisation
in a week; the domestic champions expected to replace them gained roughly as
much.

Both moves assumed the same thing — that the control holds. The first assumed it
holds against the exporter's revenue; the second assumed it holds long enough for
a domestic replacement to reach volume. They cannot both be right for long,
because the second is a statement that substitution is fast and the first is a
statement that it is slow.

## Why it moved

The substitution curve is slow at the start and then abruptly is not, which is
exactly the shape that defeats linear extrapolation.

For the first several quarters the domestic share barely moves, because the
constraint is not willingness or capital but qualification: a customer has to
re-test the part, re-certify the process and accept the yield loss. That work is
invisible in trade data. Then qualification completes across several customers at
once, and share moves in a step rather than a drift.

This is why a control looks devastating for four quarters and porous by the
eighth. Nothing changed about the policy in between. What changed is that the
qualification pipeline emptied onto the market.

## What to watch

Not export volumes, which are noisy and easily rerouted, but **qualification
announcements** — the unglamorous customer disclosures that a second source has
been approved for production. They lead the share data by two to three quarters
and they are published, because customers have to tell their own investors.

When qualifications cluster while the price premium on the controlled input is
still elevated, the premium is being paid for a denial that has already stopped
being one.
