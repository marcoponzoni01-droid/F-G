The standard account of an election trade is that the market prefers one
candidate and reprices when that candidate wins. The data rarely cooperates.

What reprices most reliably is not the identity of the winner but the *width of
the distribution*. A two-way race with a five-point gap carries a smaller premium
than a four-way race with a three-point gap, whatever the candidates propose. The
premium is paid for not knowing, and it is refunded when the not-knowing ends.

## What moved

The spread widened by nearly two hundred basis points across the campaign and
gave back three-quarters of that within a month — with the turn arriving at the
first round, not at the final result.

That timing is the whole tell. If the market were pricing a programme, the move
would come when the programme became certain. It came instead when the *field*
narrowed from four plausible outcomes to two, which is a statement about variance
rather than about policy.

## Why it moved

Mandates, not opinions, do most of the work here.

A fund with a volatility budget reduces exposure as realised and implied
volatility rise, irrespective of its view on the election. Index rebalancing is
mechanical. And local banks, which hold the largest share of most emerging
sovereign curves, shorten duration into political uncertainty because their
regulators ask them to. None of that selling is a forecast; all of it is unwound
automatically once volatility falls.

The currency behaves differently, and this is where the two get conflated. The
spread prices uncertainty and mean-reverts. The currency prices the expected
policy mix and often does not come back, because a genuine change in the fiscal
path is a change in the level, not the variance.

## What to watch

The gap between the **spread compression date** and the **inauguration date**.
When the spread has already retraced most of its campaign widening before the new
government takes office, the market has priced the end of uncertainty and not the
content of the programme — which means the actual fiscal announcements are still
ahead of it, and they are not in the price.
